## Proposed opening and positioning

For the founder who owns the experience:

> Seven relatives helped care for my great-grandmother. But only two were willing to help with bathing and diaper changes. Having seven people involved didn't mean the work was shared.

This is a draft paraphrase of the user's report, not a verbatim research quote. If another teammate speaks, say “our teammate's great-grandmother.” Retain France if context matters; do not imply the experience happened in the US. Outside nursing support did not relieve everyone's burden equally, but do not blame relatives or infer their motives.

Proposed value proposition:

> HandsIn helps families sharing care know who is responsible, what needs doing, and what happened.

Proposed close:

> Care is shared when responsibility is clear.

These are positioning proposals; ensure the chosen demonstration supports the implied product behavior.

## Customer and business argument

**Initial customer hypothesis:** an adult child coordinating care for an older relative with at least one other helper. The care recipient and helpers benefit; the coordinator/family is the proposed payer. The US is the chosen first market.

**Adoption trigger hypothesis:** new recurring care needs, a changed schedule, or the regular helper needing someone to take over. Establish the trigger with real conversations.

**Switching hypothesis:** accepted responsibilities, current instructions and attributed updates could make coordination easier than reconstructing commitments from messages. A new app also creates friction: someone must enter information, invite others and get them to return. Do not ignore that adoption cost.

**Price:** the existing brief's approximately $8 per family/circle per month is a test hypothesis, not validated willingness to pay. Decide what is included before quoting it. Do not promise unlimited AI calls or messages at an uncosted price.

**Costs to consider:** hosting, database, storage, model calls if actually used, SMS/voice if included, onboarding, support and retention. Do not invent gross margins or projections. A competitor price is context, not evidence that families will pay HandsIn.

**First customers:** propose starting with three reachable US care circles through warm personal introductions. Name actual routes and people privately with their permission before presenting them as available access. A named support-group organizer may be a later recruitment route, not an existing partner. Avoid vague “social media” and mass campus email.

**First practical step after the weekend:** observe one real coordination routine with both coordinator and helper, using non-sensitive/demo information where appropriate; record failures, assistance, repeat use and alternatives. Ask what they would pay for only after understanding the job. A pilot proposal is not customer traction.

**Why now:** no supported macro timing claim was established. Prefer a specific team insight about unequal responsibility, if confirmed, over generic claims that AI suddenly makes family coordination possible. Relevant current technology may reduce transcription work, but that does not prove demand or novelty.

**Team credibility:** connect the founder's experience and the team's actual weekend work to a concrete next step. Do not invent credentials, roles or commitments.

## Conditional live proof runbook

This is a candidate sequence, **not a verified exact click path**. The demo owner must replace it with observed labels and expected results on the submitted version.

Initial state:

- Exact submitted URL/SHA recorded; no last-minute feature changes.
- Fictional recipient and reviewed care instructions.
- Organizer and authorized helper signed in on two prepared accounts/devices.
- Visit timing compatible with actual acceptance/arrival/completion rules. Do not fake device time or bypass checks.
- Both views pre-opened, connection verified, screen zoom readable.
- No login, invite creation or account setup during the timed pitch unless that is the actual value being demonstrated.

Candidate sequence:

1. Show the coordinator's visit/task and its responsible helper.
2. On the helper's account, accept responsibility and show the current instructions.
3. Record an allowed completion/update for the prepared scenario.
4. Return to the coordinator and show the attributed result/history after the actual refresh behavior.
5. If it is quick and already verified, demonstrate persistence on reload.

The connected source refreshes every ten seconds and on focus. Budget for actual behavior; do not narrate instantaneous synchronization. Acceptance is not arrival; arrival is a self-report, not GPS proof. A recorded task outcome is not clinical proof of medication administration.

Do not stage a full replacement request, task-scoped handoff or access expiry merely because a richer separate demo has that behavior. Verify each claimed step in the submitted flow. If the strongest live slice is simpler, tell the simpler story.

For a scanner-led alternative, use an actual supported synthetic label, show extraction, correct/review fields, save, and verify the resulting shared record. Manual entry is a valid disclosed fallback but does not prove OCR. A sample button does not prove uploaded-document parsing.

## Recovery and fallback

- Have a second device open to the same submitted version with required access prepared.
- Capture actual screenshots of the main flow; label them as screenshots if used.
- Prepare a short recording of the verified flow if useful. It is supplementary backup, not the optional challenge entry.
- If a live action fails, spend no more than 15 seconds recovering.
- Suggested recovery line: “The live connection isn't responding. Here is the same flow captured from our submitted build.”
- Only use that line if the capture really is from the submitted build. Otherwise state exactly what the capture represents.
- Continue the customer story and business argument; do not debug on stage or pretend the fallback is live.
- Cut optional steps and shorten the close rather than exceeding four minutes.

## Judge Q&A priorities

Answer directly, support with evidence, acknowledge limits, then state the next step. Avoid bluffing.

| Likely question | Answer direction and evidence needed |
| --- | --- |
| What is actually working? | Name only verified submitted flows. Distinguish candidate code, local tests and hosted behavior |
| Why would a family leave its group chat? | Show the accepted responsibility/current instructions/attributed update; acknowledge adoption friction and unvalidated preference |
| Who has used it? | Give exact true counts and roles; distinguish founder experience, teammate testing, interviews and target-user use |
| Why pay? | State proposed payer and tested evidence if any; label historical $8 as a hypothesis; explain the first validation step |
| How is this different from Caring Village or Lotsa? | Acknowledge their overlapping capabilities. Demonstrate the chosen handoff emphasis; do not claim exclusivity |
| Does it really work across caregivers? | Show second account and persisted result on submitted version; do not substitute same-browser local state |
| What does AI do? | Explain Tesseract OCR and rules accurately; credit build tools separately; only name actual model integrations |
| What happens when extraction is wrong? | Show reviewed draft/correction/manual entry if verified; never claim OCR is always right |
| What does the family trust? | Explain one implemented safeguard precisely; code-level encryption/access claims need deployment verification and are not compliance certification |
| Why are you the team? | Lived experience, demonstrated work and a realistic post-weekend validation plan |
| What is your next step? | A narrow pilot/observation plan with reachable families, not an unsupported national rollout |
| Where are phone calls, NFC, paid help or payments? | Label verified status honestly; retain vision without presenting unfinished capabilities as live |

Backup material may include the verified capability matrix, exact test evidence, actual research excerpts, alternatives, and cost assumptions. Do not fill the main pitch with every caveat.

## Rehearsal and iteration

Run at least three passes if time permits:

1. Full actual clicks: record duration and every confusing or slow moment.
2. Failure drill: interrupt the demo and switch within 15 seconds.
3. Skeptical judge round: business and technical questions, concise answers, then another timed run.

Aim for roughly 3:40. Keep a shortened version that cuts optional detail. A timer estimate or word count is not a completed rehearsal.

After each pass record: version/URL, presenter, duration, slow step, unsupported claim, judge confusion, revision and remaining risk. Do not rerun unchanged checks indefinitely; focus on identified weaknesses.
